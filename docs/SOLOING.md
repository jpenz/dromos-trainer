# Soloing over the dromoi — guitar & bouzouki

The reference for improvising over the progressions in this app, in laïko, modern
bouzouki and nisiotika. Ordered by what pays off soonest.

---

## 1. The pentatonic skeleton (and the trap)

Pentatonic is the safe frame: five notes, no wrong ones, so you can think about
phrasing instead of pitches. But **each dromos needs a different pentatonic**, and
this is where most guitarists go wrong:

| Dromos | Pentatonic | Notes on D |
|---|---|---|
| Major | major pent — `1 2 3 5 6` | D E F♯ A B |
| Minor | minor pent — `1 ♭3 4 5 ♭7` | D F G A C |
| Ousak | minor pent — `1 ♭3 4 5 ♭7` | D F G A C |
| **Hijaz** | **dominant pent — `1 3 4 5 ♭7`** | **D F♯ G A C** |

> **The trap:** minor pentatonic over Hijaz is wrong. Hijaz has a **major 3rd**
> (F♯), so the ♭3 (F♮) fights the chord. If you only ever reach for the minor
> pentatonic shape, Hijaz will always sound off and you won't know why.

Note that Ousak and Minor share a pentatonic — same as they share chords. The
pentatonic can't tell them apart either. That's the point of section 2.

## 2. The flavour notes are the payoff, not the skeleton

The pentatonic deliberately omits the notes that define the mode. Those are the two
notes the app rings in orange:

| Dromos | Flavour notes on D | How they behave |
|---|---|---|
| Minor | E, F | plain |
| Ousak | **E♭**, F | ♭2 leans hard onto the tonic |
| Hijaz | **E♭**, **F♯** | ♭2 → 1, and the ♭2–3 gap is the sound |

**Use them as arrivals, not passing notes.** Run the pentatonic, then land on the ♭2
and let it resolve down a half step to the tonic. One well-placed ♭2 does more than a
scale run. The ♭6 behaves the same way — resolve it down to the 5.

## 3. Think in tetrachords, not scales

This is the biggest mindset shift, and the thing Western guitar teaching misses.
Greek and Turkish melody is built from **four-note cells**, not seven-note scales.
D Hijaz is really two cells stacked:

```
lower (Hijaz)     D   E♭  F♯  G          <- the character lives here
upper (minor)     A   B♭  C   D          <- calmer, more diatonic
```

Phrases live **inside one tetrachord**, then move to the other. That is why real
laïko lines circle around four notes and only occasionally traverse the octave.

**Drill:** improvise 8 bars using only `D E♭ F♯ G`. Then 8 bars using only
`A B♭ C D`. Then join them. Your lines will immediately sound more idiomatic than
running the full scale.

## 4. Descend

Greek melody overwhelmingly **falls toward the tonic**. Start high, come down. The
Ousak signature is a straight descent:

```
D  C  B♭  A  G  F  E♭  D
```

**Drill:** make every phrase end lower than it began. It's a crude rule and it works
immediately.

## 5. Triads through the changes

Use the **Triads** view. The rule underneath it:

| Chord | Play the triad |
|---|---|
| `maj7`, `7` | major triad |
| `m7`, `m` | minor triad |
| `m7♭5` | diminished triad |

Sevenths are colour *on top of* a triad. Over `A7` you are playing an **A major
triad** — and its C♯ is the note that makes the dominant pull.

The **3rd of each chord is the mode-definer**. If you land on one note per chord,
land on the 3rd.

**Drill (one position):** pick a hand position and play *only* the triads of the
progression there, never shifting. The app shows the least-movement path — typically
one or two fingers move per chord. `D – Gm – Cm – D` in Hijaz sits entirely at frets
3–4 in second inversion.

## 6. Target-note practice

The most efficient soloing exercise there is:

1. **One note per chord.** Choose the 3rd of each chord. Play *only* that, in time.
2. **Two notes.** Add the flavour note or the 7th.
3. **Approach it.** Arrive at the target from a half step below, on the beat.
4. **Then improvise** — but you must still hit the target on beat 1.

This is the same guide-tone idea as the ii–V–I cycle, applied to modal changes.

## 7. Ornament vocabulary

Notes alone won't sound Greek. The articulation carries the style:

- **Tremolo** on any long note — essential on bouzouki, and the main way a mandolin-
  family instrument sustains. Practise even tremolo before fast single lines.
- **Slide into the 3rd** from a half step below.
- **Grace note from the ♭2** dropping onto the tonic — the Hijaz/Ousak signature.
- **Hammer/pull inside one tetrachord** rather than picking every note.
- **Trills** on the 2nd degree — very common in nisiotika violin lines.

## 8. Play to the meter

You cannot phrase idiomatically against the wrong pulse:

| Style | Meter |
|---|---|
| Tsifteteli | 4/4 |
| Hasapiko | 2/4 |
| Zeibekiko | 9/8 (slow, rubato-ish) |
| Kalamatianos | 7/8 |
| Syrtos / Ballos / Sousta (nisiotika) | 2/4 |

Zeibekiko in particular is *felt*, not counted — phrases stretch across the bar.
Don't practise it with a 4/4 click.

## 9. Style notes

**Laïko / modern bouzouki.** Minor, Ousak and Hijaz dominate. Lines are chord-aware —
the harmony moves underneath, so triad targeting (section 5) matters. Modern playing
is faster, more chromatic and more Western-harmonic than rebetiko; heavy tremolo on
sustained notes.

**Nisiotika.** Brighter and more diatonic — major and Rast lean, often over a drone
(the tsampouna's drone is the historical reason). Ornaments are quicker and more
violin-like: trills, fast turns, less bending. Dance-driven and steady, so lock the
rhythm before decorating. Practising over a **drone** rather than changes is closer
to the real idiom here.

**Rebetiko.** Ousak and Hijaz, sparser, more vocal-like phrasing, more space.

## 10. A practice loop that works

Twenty minutes, in order:

1. **Technique** (Scale Lab, picking path) — one dromos, one position, alternate
   picking, slow enough that the inside crossings stay clean. 5 min.
2. **Audiation** (Scale Lab, cells) — 3 notes to the octave and back, singing each
   target before you play it. 5 min.
3. **Triads** — the current progression in one position, then a second position. 5 min.
4. **Improvise** — pentatonic skeleton only for one chorus, then add the flavour
   notes, then restrict yourself to one tetrachord. 5 min.

The order matters: hands, then ear, then harmony, then freedom.
