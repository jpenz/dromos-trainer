# Bouzouki mastery source model

## Answer first

Dromos treats **ta–ka alternate attack as the default engine for an articulated
line**: one written note, one deliberate pick attack. Tremolo is not an automatic
way to play every line. It is a separately selected sustain colour, introduced
after an even note-by-note attack. Glide/sweep strokes and left-hand ornaments are
also explicit phrase choices rather than hidden exceptions.

This is the operating model for Picking Lab and the future phrase curriculum. It
connects five things in order:

1. an even attack;
2. coordinated course and finger changes;
3. a practical neck route chosen for movement **and sound**;
4. dromos-specific phrase language;
5. transfer to chord targets, Greek pulse, improvisation, and repertoire.

The app does not ingest commercial books by copying their notation, drills, audio,
or phrases. It ingests a lawful **knowledge layer**: source metadata, the small
claims a public source can support, a rights boundary, and an original app exercise
derived from those domains.

## Five-stage practice spine

| Stage | Answer | Current Dromos exercises | Pass gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Ta–ka time | Build an even down/up clock; every note in an articulated line receives one attack. | One-note clock, picked dromos line, grouped accents | Upstrokes match downstrokes in time, volume, and clarity. |
| 2. Coordination | Preserve the clock while the course, finger order, and degree window change. | Two- and three-per-course crossings, four-note degree windows | A course change is no louder, later, or more tense than its neighbours. |
| 3. Neck route | Compare horizontal and tiered routes by movement and timbre. | Horizontal/tiered A/B, timbre echo, alternate/glide A/B | The player can perform two routes and name an audible reason for the choice. |
| 4. Phrase language | Keep tremolo, picked line, glide, and ornament as distinct behaviours. | Tremolo ladder, ornament chain, generic dromos contour | The target and contour remain clearer than the technique. |
| 5. Performance transfer | Make the attack reveal the chord, dromos, target, and Greek pulse. | Current-triad arpeggio to the next chord's 3rd | The arrival makes the change audible without the visual prompt. |

Exercises must change one primary variable at a time before they evolve through
positions or keys. Tempo rises only after three controlled passes. A generated
generic contour is never labelled as a Karantinis, Chiotis, Filippatos, or historic
phrase without an authorised transcription and musical review.

Publisher catalogues and public samples establish only the claims visible in those
materials. A title in the reference shelf is not evidence that Dromos has read or
licensed its full contents.

## Source-to-product rules

Every exercise carries:

- one articulation contract (`picked line`, `tremolo sustain`, `picked + legato`,
  or `picked/glide comparison`);
- one or more source IDs and visible authority labels;
- an app-authored instruction, listening goal, and measurable pass criterion;
- a plain-language boundary describing what is **not** a transcription.

Community evidence cannot stand alone. Artist names cannot be used as decoration.
When a lesson says phraseology matters, Dromos may build a generic hear–sing–play
scaffold, but it may not present that scaffold as the artist's phrase.

## Material reviewed locally

Four user-authorised practice videos in the supplied Nikos Gyras folder were
reviewed as learner-practice evidence. They are useful for planning loop, slowdown,
stroke annotation, and teacher-feedback workflows. A mid-frame visual inspection
does not prove the exact pick direction or phrase grammar, so no technical rule was
derived from those clips. Frame/audio annotation or the player's own notes would be
required before using them as a scored exercise.

## Next responsible ingestion phases

1. **Acquire and register owned/licensed material.** Record edition, instrument,
   rights, topic, level, and page/timecode metadata for Trigas, Avlonitis,
   Karantinis, Filippatos, and other method material the user lawfully owns.
2. **Annotate without copying.** Store concepts, prerequisite, failure pattern,
   degree contour, articulation, target, and source location—not scans, notation,
   song audio, or copied exercise text.
3. **Teacher validation.** Have trichordo and tetrachordo teachers review stroke
   vocabulary, fingering practicality, dromos phrase claims, and progression use.
4. **Adaptive diagnostics.** Recommend the next drill based on the failed layer:
   attack, synchronization, crossing, route, phrase, harmony target, or pulse.
5. **Production sound.** Record or license a dry, pitch-stable bouzouki sample set
   with controlled attacks, tremolo layers, and velocity variation; keep the piano
   reference for scored pitch/harmony ear checks.

This document is the human-readable contract. The executable source hierarchy and
curriculum mapping live in `js/bouzouki-knowledge.js`; regressions prevent missing
rights boundaries, unknown source IDs, or community-only rules.
